How it works

How it thinks

Four steps, repeated ten times a second.

01

Sees

A camera in its head and hearing, with blind spots, like a real animal.

02

Feels

Four needs and three emotions rise and fall: energy, company, curiosity, territory.

03

Chooses

Every behaviour gets a score. The best one wins, and the reason is written down.

04

Acts

A neural network trained in simulation moves the legs; the tail and head show the mood.

The question

Does it work?

Can a robot with a believable character be built from off-the-shelf trained legs and an explainable rule-based mind, without training any behaviour?

H1confirmed

The dog's body can be extended without retraining the walking policy

0.080 m/s mean speed error

0.072 m/s on the original Go1 model, 0.080 m/s with neck, tail, mouth and head camera added. No falls while walking in 15 runs.

H2confirmed

Behaviour without a script stays stable over long runs

72 min, 0 falls

A 3.5-minute day under 20 different seeds: 7 behaviours, 8 to 10 switches between them per day, nothing scripted.

H3confirmed in our scenarios

Guarding by time to target keeps intruders away from the protected point

0 / 3 scenarios where the point was reached

Closest approach 0.94 m in the two-intruder test. Not yet compared against simpler guarding strategies.

H4confirmed

Emotions change how the dog acts, not what it decides

4 vs 2 bounces at the greeting

Same seeds, emotions on and off: the same behaviours are chosen, but whining goes 7.5 vs 5.0 per minute and the startle 0.78 vs none.

H5not tested yet

People read the dog as alive and understand what it does

— needs a viewer study

The films with and without emotions are ready for a survey with a standard questionnaire; the study itself has not been run.

What turned out to work

  • Isolated mind: the brain sees only a perception snapshot, so the same code runs in physics and in a one-second sandbox for tests
  • Reproducible: every run replays from its seed and logs match byte for byte
  • Explainable: each decision is logged with its reasons and drawn on screen
  • Guarding geometry: threats ranked by time to target, positioning on the intruder–target line

Limits

  • Simulation only; no transfer to a real robot was attempted
  • People follow scripts or the keyboard and touch the dog only with their hands
  • The walking policy only goes forward and turns, up to 0.5 m/s
  • Perception is a sensor model over simulator truth, not a learned detector

Next steps

  • Compare guarding with simpler strategies over many seeds
  • A viewer study: does the dog with emotions look more alive?
  • Port to Unitree Go2 and try on real hardware
Conclusion

Yes, within a simulation. Trained legs plus a rule-based mind give a dog that guards, plays, rests and misses its owner on its own, runs for over an hour without a fall, and can explain every move. What is still open is how people perceive it and how it would behave on a real robot.

Specification

Simulator
MuJoCo 3.16, 2 ms physics step scene and robot assembled in code with MjSpec
Robot
Unitree Go1, 12 leg joints plus neck pan and tilt, tail lift and wag, a mouth and a head camera
Locomotion
pretrained policy, MLP 91 → 128 → 128 → 12 inference in NumPy at 50 Hz, joint PD (kp 20, kd 1) in Python
Vision
240° field of view, 12 m range occlusion by ray casting; noise 3 cm + 1 cm per metre; 5% dropouts
Hearing
intensity halves at 4 m footsteps heard within 4 m, located to about 0.35 m
Touch
5 zones: head, back, both sides, belly classified as stroke, pat or push from force and onset
Behaviour
7 modes, 3 reflexes sentry, companion, fetch, rest, explore, missing owner, idle; startle, stagger, get-up
Geometry
threats ranked by time to target interception on the Apollonius circle; distance zones with hysteresis

Engineering practice

  • Deterministic: no system clock or global random in the brain; every run replays from its seed and logs are verified byte for byte
  • Testable: the same brain runs in a kinematic sandbox that plays a scenario in about a second
  • 89 tests: pytest with property-based tests (Hypothesis) for the geometry
  • Typed: mypy --strict and ruff on every change, in CI

Explainability

  • Every tick is logged: scores of all modes, the winner and the two factors behind it
  • On screen: mode, needs, emotions, guard zones and threat lines are drawn from the brain's own state
  • 13 design records: each non-obvious decision written down with its alternatives
  • About 9,800 lines of Python in the package, 1,200 in tests

How the films are made

  • Simulate once: joint positions at 50 Hz and the brain state at 10 Hz are saved as a take
  • Film many times: a separate render-only model of the room, camera rigs, slow motion and frozen time by interpolation
  • Post-processing: depth of field from the depth buffer, bloom, grade; text in two languages
  • Physics untouched: the render model only adds what is drawn, so the logs and metrics stay identical
