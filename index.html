Pet or Threat

A Unitree Go1 robot dog in a simulated living room. It decides for itself when to play, when to guard and when to sleep, and shows why, right on screen.

How it works

How it thinks

Four steps, repeated ten times a second.

01

Sees

A camera in its head and hearing, with blind spots, like a real animal.

02

Feels

Four needs and three emotions rise and fall: energy, company, curiosity, territory.

03

Chooses

Every behaviour gets a score. The best one wins, and the reason is written down.

04

Acts

A neural network trained in simulation moves the legs; the tail and head show the mood.

Its reasoning, straight from the logchase ball: fetch 0.90 (ball 1.00, energy 0.90) > companion 0.53

For engineers
  • MuJoCo physics at 500 Hz, the Unitree Go1 model, with a neck, tail, mouth, head camera and touch sensors added.
  • Learned legs: walking policies trained with reinforcement learning, run as a small neural network in NumPy.
  • Rule-based brain: needs as differential equations, emotions with habituation, a utility arbiter and a behaviour tree per mode.
  • Honest perception: field of view, occlusion, noise and dropouts; the brain never reads the simulator.
  • Deterministic: every scenario replays identically from a seed; logs can be verified byte for byte.
  • Quality: 89 automated tests, strict typing, 13 written design decisions.
